What each terminal sends for the keys of the Modern style
=========================================================

Measured on one machine, 2026-11-14, with a raw reader in the terminal (put
the tty in raw mode, send the two keyboard-protocol requests the editor
sends, then log every burst of bytes), the key pressed with xdotool under
Xvfb with a window manager.  "legacy" is the terminal with no request sent;
"pushed" is after "CSI > 4 ; 2 m" (xterm modifyOtherKeys 2) and
"CSI > 5 u" (kitty flags 1 and 4).  The reader then asks "CSI ? u" (kitty)
and "CSI ? 4 m" (xterm) and records the reply.  wpe_key_delivery[] in
we_bindings.c is filled from this file and from nothing else.

The reader and its driver are test scaffolding, not shipped; the editor's own
decoder was then fed the byte strings below (tests/test_keys_delivery.py,
the konsole and tmux rows).

Terminals and versions
----------------------
xterm 412, kitty 0.48.2, Konsole 26.08.3, GNOME Terminal 3.60.0 (VTE
0.84.1), xfce4-terminal 1.2.0 (VTE), tmux 3.7c, GNU screen 5.0.2.

Not run: the Linux console (no console to drive; the editor sends no request
there, see TIOCLINUX in we_term.c), macOS Terminal.app and iTerm2 (no Mac),
foot (it needs a Wayland compositor; Wayland is a display of its own, not a
terminal here).

What could not be driven, said plainly
--------------------------------------
- kitty: the window never received the key events under Xvfb (a window
  manager and an explicit focus were both tried), so only its replies to the
  two requests are measured.  Its key forms are the kitty protocol's, from
  its specification, not from this machine.
- GNOME Terminal and xfce4-terminal: they started and ran the reader, but no
  key event reached them under Xvfb, so only their replies are measured: none
  to either request, which means neither protocol.  Their Ctrl-PgUp/PgDn,
  Ctrl-Shift-C/V/T/W/N/Q/F and F1/F10/F11 are the tab and menu keys the
  reference chapter already names; they are DOCUMENTED here, not measured.
- tmux without extended-keys: the log lost its alignment (tmux answers its own
  start-up queries first), so the "legacy" column of tmux is not used.
- screen: Ctrl-S is its flow control (XOFF) and nothing arrives for it; the
  rows after it are shifted by one in the log.  Read as: screen answered
  neither request, and Ctrl-Shift-letter arrives as Ctrl-letter.
- Every function key carries a modifier 3 in the X terminals here (xterm and
  Konsole alike), and Ctrl-F5, Ctrl-F10 and Ctrl-Alt-Down never arrive: that
  is the Xvfb keymap and the window manager, not the terminals.  The F-key
  rows are therefore not used as measurements of the terminals; the decoder's
  rows for them stand on the xterm tables (we_keymap.c).

Which protocol each terminal implements (the replies)
-----------------------------------------------------
  xterm 412          modifyOtherKeys: yes.  Reply to "CSI ? 4 m" is
                     "CSI > 4 ; 0 m" before the request and "CSI > 4 ; 2 m"
                     after it.  Kitty protocol: no reply.
  kitty 0.48.2       kitty protocol: yes.  Reply "CSI ? 0 u" before,
                     "CSI ? 5 u" after the request.  modifyOtherKeys: no
                     reply.
  Konsole 26.08.3    kitty protocol: yes ("CSI ? 0 u" before, "CSI ? 5 u"
                     after).  modifyOtherKeys: no reply.
  GNOME Terminal     neither (no reply to either).
  3.60.0 / VTE 0.84
  xfce4-terminal     neither (VTE, no reply to either).
  1.2.0
  tmux 3.7c          no reply to either request, but with extended-keys on
                     (and terminal-features extkeys) it passes the xterm
                     form "CSI 27 ; m ; c ~" to an application that asked for
                     modifyOtherKeys 2.  With extended-keys off it passes the
                     legacy bytes.
  GNU screen 5.0.2   neither (no reply); legacy bytes only.

Two facts the table shows that the design did not know:
- Konsole in the kitty protocol reports "CSI 115::59;5u": the shifted-key
  field is empty and the third field is the key of the base layout.  The
  decoder reads the first field and ignores the other two, which the
  decoder's rows (tests/test_keys_delivery.py) prove on these exact bytes.
- Konsole and xterm differ on the plain Backspace chords: xterm keeps Ctrl-
  Backspace as 0x08 even under modifyOtherKeys, Konsole reports it as
  "CSI 127;5u".  The Modern map therefore binds Ctrl-Backspace from the
  protocol form and keeps Alt-Backspace as the fallback (it is a legacy byte
  pair, ESC DEL, in every terminal).

How this fills wpe_key_delivery[]
---------------------------------
ALWAYS (a legacy byte or a plain CSI every terminal sends, unaffected by the
protocols): Ctrl-S, Ctrl-Z, Ctrl-Y, Ctrl-O, Ctrl-A, Ctrl-D, Ctrl-F, Ctrl-G,
Ctrl-R, Ctrl-N, Ctrl-Q, Ctrl-W, Ctrl-/ (as 0x1f), Ctrl-Space (as NUL), the
cursor block with Shift, Ctrl and Alt, Ctrl-Delete, Ctrl-Home/End, Ctrl-PgUp/
PgDn, Esc, Alt-letter.
PROTOCOL (a distinct key only where the terminal implements a protocol, which
among the terminals measured is xterm (modifyOtherKeys), Konsole and kitty
(kitty): every Ctrl-Shift-letter, Ctrl-Tab, Ctrl-`, Ctrl-H and Ctrl-[ as
keys of their own, Ctrl-Backspace).  tmux passes the xterm form with
extended-keys on.  GNOME Terminal, xfce4-terminal and screen implement
neither, so these keys are TAKEN there by lack of a protocol.
TAKEN (the terminal keeps the key): Konsole takes every Ctrl-Shift-letter and
Ctrl-Tab (measured); GNOME Terminal and xfce4-terminal take Ctrl-PgUp/PgDn,
Ctrl-Shift-C/V/T/W/N/Q/F, F1, F10, F11 and Alt-1..9 (documented, not
measured); kitty takes its own Ctrl-Shift set and Ctrl-Tab (documented); tmux
takes Ctrl-B (its prefix, documented); screen takes Ctrl-S (measured, XOFF);
the window manager takes Alt-F4.
